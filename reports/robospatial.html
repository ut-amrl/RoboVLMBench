RoboSpatial-Home

Spatial understanding for robots: 350 questions on photos of home scenes, scored with the official evaluation.

What each category tests

  • Configuration (123 yes/no questions): where objects are relative to each other. E.g. “Is the picture above the desk? Answer yes or no.”
  • Compatibility (105 yes/no questions): whether an object fits in a place. E.g. “Can the tissue box fit behind the chair? Answer yes or no.”
  • Context (122 pointing questions): point to free space described relative to an object; correct if one of the first two points lands in the annotated free space. E.g. “In the image, there is a bowl. Pinpoint several points within the vacant space situated to the left of the bowl.”

% correct. VQA avg is the mean of configuration and compatibility; Total is the mean of all three categories (the leaderboard's definitions). With 105–123 questions per category, one category's score is uncertain by up to ±10 points (95% interval), so smaller differences may be chance. The 350 questions share 42 photos. Time per question: from sending a question to receiving the whole answer (reasoning included), with questions sent one at a time and nothing else on the GPUs. Every image is sent as new, so the server can't reuse work from earlier questions on the same image, as for a robot whose next question comes with a new camera frame. Output tokens: median per question, reasoning included. Unusable answers: cut off at the token limit, unparsable, or failed (hover for which); they count as wrong.

Yes/no bias

Accuracy can hide a lean toward one answer: 83% of compatibility questions have the answer “yes”, so always answering “yes” scores 82.9 there. Balanced accuracy is the mean of the accuracy on “yes” questions and on “no” questions: 50 for any constant answer, 100 for a perfect one.
