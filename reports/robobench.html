RoboBench

Robot “system-2” reasoning (paper): perception, affordances, error analysis and planning, on photos and robot videos. The 3,087 questions of its 6,092 with deterministic scoring, asked and scored as in the official evaluation.

What is included

  • RoboBench-MCQ (1,895 multiple-choice questions, A–D; the official setting of that name): perception reasoning, affordance reasoning and error analysis, 13 subtasks.
  • State estimation (1,192 yes/no questions, Planning Q3): given a robot video up to now and the plan's steps so far, is the step finished? 10 planning subtasks.
  • Not included: the other 3,005 planning questions, where the model writes a multi-step plan (Q1, 2,163, incl. navigation) or names the next step (Q2, 842). The official evaluation scores those only with an LLM judge (GPT-4o), which this harness does not call.

Prompts are the official ones: task definitions from the released system prompts, the question, the options, and “Please answer only one word, e.g., A, B, C, or D.” (state questions: “Please only answer one word, yes or no.”, after the robot's skill list and worked examples). Every image is resized to 512×512 and sent as JPEG, as the official code does. Videos are the released frames, in order: 10–34 per multiple-choice video question, 1–20 per state question. As in the official code, the text comes first, then the images.

Scoring: an answer that is only a letter (or letters) is read by the official rule; it must match the answer exactly. The official code asks GPT-4o to pick the letter out of any other answer, and to pick “yes” or “no” out of every state answer; here fixed rules do that (e.g. “B. Exo”, “The answer is B”, “Yes, it is finished.”), and answers they can't read count as wrong.

% correct. Each score is the mean of its subtasks' accuracies (the official scores are per subtask; its aggregator averages over tasks). RoboBench-MCQ: the 13 multiple-choice subtasks; chance is 25. State estimation: the 10 subtasks of yes/no questions; chance is 50. With 1,895 multiple-choice questions, RoboBench-MCQ is uncertain by about ±2 points (95% interval); single subtasks (82–367 questions) by up to ±11. Time per question: from sending a question to receiving the whole answer (reasoning included), with questions sent one at a time and nothing else on the GPUs. Every image is sent as new, so the server can't reuse work from earlier questions on the same image, as for a robot whose next question comes with a new camera frame. Output tokens: median per question, reasoning included. Unusable answers: cut off at the token limit, unparsable, or failed (hover for which); they count as wrong.
